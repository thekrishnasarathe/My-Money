1The basics

What is MONEY?

The short answer, before anything else.

MONEY (shown on your home screen simply as "MONEY", internally called My-Money) is a personal finance manager that lives entirely on your device. No account to create, no server that has to be up, no internet required to log a purchase.

You open it, you add what you spent or earned, and it quietly keeps score — your balance, your budgets, your savings goals, your recurring bills — all calculated instantly and stored in a real local database, not a spreadsheet pretending to be an app.

It's built by one person, Krishna Sarathe, top to bottom: the database, the design, the security, the PDF statements, all of it.

Offline-first PIN + biometric locked Free of ads & tracking clutter Exports real PDF statements

3Getting the most from it

How to use MONEY well

The app works the moment you open it — but a few habits make it genuinely useful instead of just another tracker you abandon in a week.

1

Set your PIN first

Before anything else, lock the app down from Settings. It takes ten seconds and means your financial data is protected from the very first entry.

2

Log the same day, not the same week

Add expenses right after you spend, not from memory later. The categorization and tags only stay useful if the entry is accurate.

Takes under 10 seconds per entry
3

Set budgets on your top 3 categories first

Don't budget everything on day one. Start with food, transport, and one more that tends to run away from you — expand once those feel automatic.

4

Put every subscription into Recurring

Every bill you pay on autopilot — rent, streaming, gym — belongs here. This is what makes your balance actually predictive instead of just historical.

5

Check Analytics weekly, not daily

Daily numbers are noise. A weekly glance at the category breakdown is where the real patterns — and the real surprises — show up.

6

Export a PDF statement monthly

Even if you don't need it today, a monthly export builds a paper trail you'll be glad to have for taxes, disputes, or just your own records.

7Making sense of it

What the structure means

Folders aren't arbitrary — each one has exactly one job. Tap to expand.

🗄️
database/

The persistence layer. One file, db_helper.dart, owns every SQL table and every migration. If your data needs to survive an app restart, it passes through here.

🧩
models/

The shapes of your data — what an Expense is, what an Income is. No logic lives here, just definitions everything else agrees on.

🔌
repositories/

The middleman between raw SQL and the app's state. Nothing above this layer writes a database query directly — it all goes through here first.

⚡
providers/

The reactive brain. When you add an expense, this is what notices, recalculates your balance, and tells every screen watching it to redraw.

🖼️
screens/

Everything you actually see and tap — dashboard, transactions, analytics, goals, recurring, settings. One subfolder per screen.

🛰️
services/

Everything that talks to the outside world or the OS — Firebase, local notifications, device session identity.

🎨
utils/

Shared reference material — theme colors, global constants, and the PDF export engine. Anything referenced from everywhere lives here.

🧱
widgets/

Reusable visual pieces — buttons, cards, chips — built once and used across every screen so the app looks consistent everywhere.

9Trust & safety

How your data is protected

Financial data deserves real security, not a token password field. Here's exactly what's in place.

Your PIN is never stored as-is. It's run through SHA-256 together with a random value unique to your device — called a salt — so even if someone got access to the stored value, they couldn't reverse it back into your PIN, and the same PIN looks completely different on two different devices.

stored_value = SHA256(pin + device_salt)

On top of that sits biometric unlock — fingerprint or FaceID, handled by your phone's own secure hardware rather than the app itself.

The main risks were mapped out and mitigated deliberately:

Someone brute-forcing your PIN
Higher risk
Each wrong attempt adds a longer delay, with biometric lockout as a backstop.
Unauthorized cloud access
Higher risk
Firestore rules only ever allow a user to read or write their own data — never anyone else's.
Someone stealing your device
Moderate
Data sits behind the OS sandbox and your salted PIN, out of easy reach even with the phone in hand.
Data intercepted in transit
Moderate
Every bit of cloud sync travels over encrypted HTTPS (TLS 1.3) — nothing leaves in the clear.
